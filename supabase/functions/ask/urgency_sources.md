# When to get help: where each level comes from

The warning signs Miracle AI can show (`urgency.ts`), each with the public guidance its level
rests on (checked October 29, 2026; 69 pages opened). Drafted from public medical guidance, always
taking the more urgent level when sources differ or it is borderline. **Not yet reviewed by a
clinician**: the card says so until someone has. Notes below flag the calls to look at first.

"Call 911 or go to the ER now" also covers guidance that says "call 911 or go to the emergency
room" (NHS: "call 999 or go to A&E"); "Same day" covers "call your provider right away" and urgent
care; "This week" covers "contact your provider" and routine visits.

## Call 911 or go to the ER now

- **Chest pain, pressure or tightness, or pain spreading to the arm, neck, jaw or back** (`chest_pain`): <https://cdc.gov/heart-disease/about/heart-attack.html>, <https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/001927.htm>  
  Supported. CDC (updated Oct 2024): chest pain or discomfort, or pain in the jaw, neck, back, arms or shoulders, call 911 immediately. MedlinePlus (reviewed Jan 2025) lists chest pain lasting 2 minutes or more as an emergency sign. Keeping "spreading" ties arm, neck or back pain to the chest, so post-workout back pain alone does not read as a 911 sign; CDC lists those areas as symptoms on their own, so a clinician may want them standalone.
- **Trouble breathing, or sudden shortness of breath** (`breathing`): <https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/001927.htm>, <https://www.emergencyphysicians.org/siteassets/files/pdfs/isitanemergency.pdf>  
  Supported. MedlinePlus "Recognizing medical emergencies" and ACEP both list difficulty breathing or shortness of breath as emergency warning signs. Also the main now-level sign for a clot reaching the lungs (hormones), together with chest_pain.
- **Swelling of the lips, tongue or throat, trouble swallowing, or hives with trouble breathing or feeling faint** (`allergic`): <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/Anaphylaxis>, <https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000844.htm>  
  Level supported: NHS (reviewed Jun 2023) says call 999 for sudden swelling of the lips, mouth, throat or tongue, a tight throat or struggling to swallow, struggling to breathe, or fainting. MedlinePlus (reviewed Jan 2026) says call 911 or go to the ER. The reword adds a tight throat or trouble swallowing, which people often notice before visible swelling. FDA (Apr 2026, bulk drug substances page) flags immunogenicity risk for compounded BPC-157 and TB-500, which matters for this audience. This id is one of INJECT_SIGNS, so the reword also shows on the inject card.
- **Sudden face drooping, numbness or weakness on one side, slurred speech, confusion, or trouble walking** (`stroke`): <https://www.nichd.nih.gov/health/topics/stroke/conditioninfo/symptoms>, <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/headaches/>  
  Level supported: NIH/NICHD says call 911 immediately for sudden numbness or weakness, especially on one side, confusion, trouble talking, trouble walking or loss of balance, and trouble seeing. NHS headaches (Apr 2024) says call 999 or go to A&E for numbness, weakness or trouble speaking with a headache. The draft says "arm or leg weakness" without "sudden" or "one side", which can be misread as weakness after a workout. It also leaves out numbness and balance, and starts with a diagnosis word. Sudden trouble seeing is covered by vision_loss.
- **A sudden, severe headache, the worst you’ve had** (`worst_headache`): <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/headaches/>, <https://www.nichd.nih.gov/health/topics/stroke/conditioninfo/symptoms>  
  Supported. NHS: a headache that started suddenly and is extremely painful means call 999 or go to A&E. NIH/NICHD lists a sudden severe headache with no known cause as a stroke sign (call 911).
- **A seizure, or someone who passes out and can’t be woken up** (`seizure`): <https://www.cdc.gov/epilepsy/first-aid-for-seizures/index.html>, <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/fainting/>  
  Level supported: CDC (May 2024) says call 911 for a first seizure, one lasting longer than 5 minutes, repeated seizures, trouble breathing or waking afterward, or a seizure in someone with diabetes or who is pregnant. For this audience most seizures will be first ones or drug-related. CDC also says seizures do not always need 911 (for example in known epilepsy); the more urgent level is kept per the owner rule. The reword adds unresponsiveness, which no entry covered. NHS fainting (Aug 2026) and NHS anaphylaxis both say call 999 if someone cannot be woken up.
- **A racing, pounding or irregular heartbeat with chest pain, trouble breathing or feeling faint** (`heartbeat_red`): <https://nhs.uk/conditions/heart-palpitations/>, <https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003081.htm>  
  Level supported. DUPLICATE: if faint is raised to now (proposed), the draft text "Fainting with chest pain..." falls entirely inside faint. The reword turns this id into heart racing with red flags, which no now-level entry covered. NHS palpitations (Mar 2026) says call 999 or go to A&E for palpitations with chest pain, shortness of breath, or feeling faint or fainting. MedlinePlus palpitations (Jun 2026) says call 911 for palpitations with chest pain, shortness of breath or dizziness. If the reviewer keeps faint at today, keep the draft text instead and add NHS's other 999 triggers: fainting during exercise or while lying down.
- **Shaking, sweating or confusion that’s no better 15 minutes after something sugary, or too drowsy to eat** (`low_sugar_severe`): <https://medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000085.htm>, <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/hypoglycaemia/>  
  Level supported. MedlinePlus "Low blood sugar - self-care" (Jan 2026) says wait about 15 minutes and recheck; if symptoms do not improve after a sugary snack, go to the ER or call 911. It also says get help right away if the person is not alert or cannot be woken up. NHS (Aug 2023) says call 999 if someone with very low blood sugar is not responding normally or is unconscious. The draft gives no time to wait, and leaves out the person who is too drowsy to eat or drink. Relevant when GLP-1s are combined with insulin or sulfonylureas.
- **Vomiting or coughing up blood, or vomit that looks like coffee grounds** (`vomit_blood`): <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/vomiting-blood/>, <https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/001927.htm>  
  Level supported. NHS (Aug 2025): vomiting blood with feeling faint, dizzy, confused, generally unwell, black stools or belly pain means call 999 or go to A&E. If the vomiting has stopped and there are no other symptoms, NHS says urgent GP or 111; the more urgent level is kept per the owner rule. MedlinePlus and ACEP list "coughing up or vomiting blood" as an emergency sign, so coughing up blood is added. It was missing, and it is a possible sign of a clot in the lungs for people on hormones.
- **Can’t pass stool or gas, with vomiting or a swollen, hard or very tender belly** (`bowel_block`): <https://www.medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003120.htm>, <https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/215256s030lbl.pdf>  
  GLP-1 medicines. MedlinePlus abdominal pain (Jan 2026): get emergency care right away or call 911 if you cannot pass stool, especially with vomiting, or your belly is tender, rigid and hard. The Wegovy label lists ileus, intestinal obstruction and severe constipation as postmarketing reports. Keeps the mild, week-level stomach entry from being picked for a blockage.
- **Bleeding that won’t stop with firm pressure (even a nosebleed), or passing a lot of blood from your rectum** (`bleeding`): <https://www.mayoclinic.org/first-aid/first-aid-severe-bleeding/basics/art-20056661>, <https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/bleeding-from-the-bottom-rectal-bleeding/>  
  Level supported. Mayo first aid: call 911 if a wound is deep or you are not sure how serious it is. MedlinePlus lists "bleeding that will not stop" as an emergency. The reword takes in heavy rectal bleeding, which the draft bundled into black_stool (today). NHS (Apr 2023) says go to A&E or call 999 for non-stop bleeding or a lot of blood, such as red toilet water or large clots. NHS nosebleed (Dec 2023) says go to A&E if a nosebleed lasts more than 10 to 15 minutes, and many people do not think of pinching the nose as "firm pressure".
- **Took too much of a medicine, peptide or supplement (if you feel fine, call Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222)** (`overdose`): <https://www.mayoclinic.org/first-aid/first-aid-poisoning/basics/art-20056657>, <https://www.poison.org/need-immediate-assistance>  
  Level supported. Mayo poisoning first aid: call 911 if the person is drowsy or unconscious, has trouble breathing, is very agitated, has seizures, or is known to have overdosed; call Poison Help if the person is stable with no symptoms. Poison Control (poison.org) says the same. "Far too much" can stop someone who made a 2 to 10 times dosing mistake from calling. FDA (Jul 2024) reported patients getting 5 to 20 times the intended dose of compounded semaglutide, some needing hospital care. The reword says when Poison Control is the right first call.
- **After vomiting, diarrhea or not drinking: confusion, being very sleepy, fast breathing, or cold, blotchy skin** (`dehydration_severe`): <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/Dehydration/>, <https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/215256s030lbl.pdf>  
  GLP-1 stomach side effects. NHS dehydration (May 2026): call 999 or go to A&E for dehydration with confusion, being sleepier than normal or hard to wake, fast breathing, cold skin, or blue, grey, pale or blotchy skin. The Wegovy label (Jan 2026) tells prescribers to monitor kidney function when side effects could cause fluid loss. Sits above fluids (today) and little_urine (today).
- **Very ill with an infection: confused or slurred speech, breathing very fast, or blotchy, pale or bluish skin** (`sepsis`): <https://www.nhs.uk/content-widgets/health-a-to-z/sepsis>, <https://www.cdc.gov/sepsis/pdfs/Drop-in-Article-for-Patients-Families-2022-508.pdf>  
  Injected peptides and research chemicals. NHS sepsis (May 2026): for adults, call 999 or go to A&E for very fast breathing, confusion or slurred speech, blue, pale or blotchy skin, a very high or low temperature or shivering, or a rash that does not fade when pressed. CDC: with an infection that is not getting better, get medical care immediately. The non-fading rash was left out for length; a clinician may want it in. Above injection_site (today) and fever (now).
- **Sudden loss of all or part of your vision, sudden severe eye pain, or a red eye with halos around lights** (`vision_loss`): <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/vision-loss/>, <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/glaucoma/>  
  Level supported and widened. NHS vision loss (Aug 2025): go to A&E or call 999 if you suddenly cannot see from one or both eyes, or suddenly have severe eye pain. NHS glaucoma (Jun 2025): call 999 or go to A&E if glaucoma symptoms come on suddenly (intense eye pain, red eye, rainbow circles around lights, headache). Both were bundled into eye (today). "All or part" matters because sudden partial loss (for example the optic-nerve stroke listed on the sildenafil label) still needs urgent care. The Viagra label (12/2017) says stop and call a provider right away for sudden vision loss; NHS and NIH are more urgent, so the more urgent level is kept.
- **An erection that lasts more than 4 hours** (`erection`): <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/priapism/>, <https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2017/020895s048lbl.pdf>  
  Supported. NHS (Dec 2023): call 999 or go to A&E for an erection lasting more than 3 to 4 hours. The Viagra label (12/2017) says get medical help right away if it lasts more than 4 hours. The FDA 4-hour threshold is kept; NHS uses "3 to 4 hours", so a clinician may prefer the earlier figure.
- **Fainting (passing out), even if you come round quickly** (`faint`): <https://www.emergencyphysicians.org/article/know-when-to-go/fainting>, <https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003092.htm>  
  RAISE (sources differ). ACEP "Know when to go": if someone faints or appears to be fainting, call 911. MedlinePlus (May 2025): call 911 if the person who fainted is over 50, has diabetes, is pregnant, has chest pain or an irregular heartbeat, or is slow to come round; otherwise see a provider. NHS (Aug 2026) says a faint with full recovery is a non-urgent GP visit, with 999 only for red flags. The card cannot know age or diabetes, and many GLP-1 users have diabetes, so the owner rule points to now. This makes faint_red a duplicate (see that entry).
- **Severe belly pain that won’t go away or spreads to your back, or belly pain with fever or yellow eyes** (`belly_pain`): <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/acute-pancreatitis/symptoms>, <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/gallstones/symptoms>  
  RAISE. NHS acute pancreatitis (Mar 2026): sudden severe tummy pain spreading to the back means call 999 or go to A&E. NHS gallstones (Aug 2025): sudden severe tummy pain, pain spreading to the back with vomiting, a very high temperature or shivering, or yellow skin or eyes means call 999 or go to A&E. MedlinePlus abdominal pain (Jan 2026): get emergency care or call 911 for sudden sharp belly pain or a hard, tender belly. The Zepbound label (Feb 2026) describes pancreatitis as persistent or severe pain, sometimes going to the back. The draft wording names exactly the NHS 999 pattern (pain going through to the back). The reword drops "with or without vomiting" (it adds nothing), and adds fever and yellow eyes from NHS gallstones. NHS puts severe pain that keeps coming back, without those features, at urgent GP; the new gallbladder entry (today) covers belly pain lasting more than 30 minutes.
- **Fever or shaking chills if you inject anything, or with a red, swollen or painful patch of skin** (`fever`): <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/Cellulitis>, <https://www.nhs.uk/content-widgets/health-a-to-z/sepsis>  
  RAISE (sources differ). NHS cellulitis (Apr 2024): cellulitis with a very high temperature, or feeling hot, cold or shivery, means call 999 or go to A&E. NHS sepsis (May 2026) lists a very high or very low temperature, or being shivery, as a reason to call 999 or go to A&E. NHS skin abscess and NHS sprains put shivering with a local infection at 111. CDC sepsis says get medical care immediately. Per the owner rule this is now. The reword ties it to injecting or a skin infection, so an ordinary cold or flu is not a 911 sign. This id is one of INJECT_SIGNS, so raising it also moves fever under "Call 911 now" on the inject card; the reword was written to read correctly there.
- **After an injury: a joint out of place or at an odd angle, can’t put weight on it, or numb, cold or blue skin** (`injury_swelling`): <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sprains-and-strains/>, <https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/joint-pain/>  
  RAISE the part that needs it (bundle split). NHS sprains (Apr 2024): call 999 or go to A&E if the injured part changed shape or is at an odd angle, is numb or tingling, or is blue, grey or cold. NHS joint pain (Feb 2026): go to A&E or call 999 if a joint has moved out of place, you cannot walk or put weight on a joint, or have no feeling after an injury. Fast swelling and heavy bruising (NHS: 111) moved to tendon_rupture (today). "Can't put weight on it" is borderline: NHS sprains and NHS broken ankle put it at 111. The id no longer matches the content; consider renaming it later (for example injury_severe) and keeping the old id as an alias.
- **A spreading rash that blisters or peels, or sores in the mouth or eyes, after starting something new** (`rash_severe`): <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/stevens-johnson-syndrome/>, <https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/stevens-johnson-syndrome/symptoms-causes/syc-20355936>  
  RAISE. NHS Stevens-Johnson syndrome (Mar 2026): call 999 or go to A&E for a rash that is blistered or peeling, or a swollen or sore mouth, lips, throat or eyes, after an infection or a new medicine. Mayo: SJS needs immediate medical attention; seek emergency care. Drug reactions can start during use or up to two weeks after stopping. "After starting something new" (medicine, peptide or supplement) keeps poison-ivy blisters from reading as a 911 sign.

## Same day: ER or urgent care

- **A racing, pounding or irregular heartbeat that’s new, or that doesn’t settle when you rest** (`heartbeat`): <https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003081.htm>, <https://nhs.uk/conditions/heart-palpitations/>  
  Level kept (sources differ). MedlinePlus (Jun 2026): contact your provider right away for new or different palpitations, or a resting pulse over 100. NHS (Mar 2026): see a GP (non-urgent) if palpitations last longer than a few minutes or keep coming back. The Wegovy label asks patients to tell their provider about a racing heartbeat. The reword spells out MedlinePlus's "new" and "at rest" triggers; the draft's "doesn't settle" left open "after what?". With chest pain, breathlessness or faintness, use faint_red (now).
- **A home blood pressure reading of 180/120 or higher that’s still that high after resting a few minutes** (`bp_high`): <https://www.mayoclinic.org/hypertensive-crisis/expert-answers/faq-20058491>  
  Hormones and stimulant-type drugs. Mayo (hypertensive crisis): if a home reading is 180/120 or higher with no symptoms, relax a few minutes and recheck; if still very high, seek medical care. With chest pain, shortness of breath or stroke signs, call 911 (chest_pain, breathing and stroke cover that). Only one source opened. PT-141 and some testosterone products carry blood-pressure warnings in their labels (not opened here; verify before citing).
- **Black, tarry or bloody stools** (`black_stool`): <https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003130.htm>, <https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/bleeding-from-the-bottom-rectal-bleeding/>  
  Supported. MedlinePlus "Black or tarry stools" (Apr 2026): contact your provider right away for blood or a change in stool color, or dizziness. NHS (Apr 2023): urgent GP or 111 for black or dark red poo, or bloody diarrhea. NHS puts "a lot of blood" or non-stop bleeding at A&E or 999, so that part is now named in bleeding (now). With belly pain or dizziness, MedlinePlus abdominal pain says get emergency care (see belly_pain and faint). Sign wording unchanged.
- **Vomiting or diarrhea so you can’t keep fluids down, or very dark urine with dizziness** (`fluids`): <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/Dehydration/>, <https://www.medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003120.htm>  
  Supported (sources differ, more urgent kept). NHS dehydration (May 2026): urgent GP or 111 for dizziness on standing that does not go away, dark yellow pee or peeing less, or a fast heart rate. MedlinePlus abdominal pain lists "inability to keep food or liquids down" under "contact your provider" without "right away". Severe dehydration signs (confusion, very sleepy, fast breathing, cold or blotchy skin) are NHS call 999 or A&E, so a new now-level dehydration_severe entry is proposed.
- **Yellow skin or yellow whites of the eyes, or dark urine with pale stools** (`jaundice`): <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/Jaundice>, <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/gallstones/symptoms>  
  Level supported. NHS jaundice (Jan 2024): urgent GP or 111 if your skin or the whites of your eyes look yellow, possibly with darker pee and paler poo. NHS notes yellowing is harder to see on brown or black skin, but the whites of the eyes still look yellow; the reword says so. With belly pain or fever, NHS gallstones says call 999 or go to A&E, which is covered by the reworded belly_pain.
- **Severe muscle pain or weakness with dark, cola-colored urine** (`muscle_urine`): <https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/21184-rhabdomyolysis>, <https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/54355/cdc_54355_DS1.pdf>  
  Supported. Cleveland Clinic: see a provider right away; rhabdomyolysis can be life-threatening and needs immediate medical attention. CDC/NIOSH (2018): seek medical evaluation right away, or go to the nearest medical center immediately. Neither says 911 or ER, so today fits. Cleveland describes the urine as brown, red or tea-colored, so "tea- or cola-colored" is an optional tweak. NIOSH also lists pain much worse than expected for the effort, which helps gym users tell it from normal soreness.
- **Swelling or throbbing pain in one leg, often the calf or thigh, sometimes with warm, red or darker skin** (`calf`): <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/deep-vein-thrombosis-dvt/>  
  Level supported. NHS DVT (Apr 2026): urgent GP or 111 if you think you have DVT, with throbbing pain or swelling in one leg (usually the calf or thigh) and red, blue or darkened skin. Call 999 or A&E if breathless or with chest pain, which breathing and chest_pain cover. The reword adds the thigh and "darker skin", since redness is hard to see on dark skin. Relevant to testosterone and estrogen users. Only one source was opened for this entry.
- **Where you injected: redness, warmth or swelling that spreads, a painful lump, or pus** (`injection_site`): <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/Cellulitis>, <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/skin-abscess/>  
  Supported. NHS cellulitis (Apr 2024): urgent GP or 111 if skin is painful, hot and swollen. NHS skin abscess (Oct 2026): urgent GP or 111 for a lump that is painful, red or hot, or redness spreading from it. With fever or shivering, NHS cellulitis says call 999 or go to A&E (see fever). A hot, swollen joint near a local injection site also belongs here (NHS joint pain: urgent GP).
- **After an injury: a pop or snap, fast swelling or heavy bruising, or sudden weakness pushing off or gripping** (`tendon_rupture`): <https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/21703-achilles-tendon-rupture>, <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sprains-and-strains/>  
  Level supported. Cleveland Clinic (Achilles rupture): see a provider right away for a pop, sudden pain, sudden swelling and bruising, or not being able to push off. NHS sprains (Apr 2024): 111 or urgent treatment centre for a large amount of swelling or bruising. The draft "A pop or snap" alone can be misread as harmless joint cracking. The reword anchors it to an injury and takes in injury_swelling's swelling and bruising. "Unable to bear weight" moved to injury_swelling (now) because NHS joint pain says A&E or 999 (NHS sprains says 111). Borderline: NHS sprains says call 999 or go to A&E if you heard a crack at the injury, and lay people may not tell a crack from a pop.
- **Peeing much less than usual, or not at all** (`little_urine`): <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/acute-kidney-injury/>, <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/Dehydration/>  
  Supported. NHS acute kidney injury (Mar 2026): urgent GP or 111 for peeing a lot less than usual. NHS dehydration: urgent GP or 111 for peeing less than normal. The Wegovy and Zepbound labels tell prescribers to watch kidney function when side effects could cause fluid loss. Possible misread: "not at all" could be taken as needing to pee but being unable to (urinary retention). NHS treats that as an emergency and no entry covers it, which is relevant for older men on testosterone. NHS sepsis also lists not peeing all day as a sepsis symptom (see the new sepsis entry).
- **New blurred vision, flashes of light, or an eye that’s red, sore or sensitive to light** (`eye`): <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/vision-loss/>, <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/glaucoma/>  
  Level supported for what stays (bundle split). NHS vision loss (Aug 2025): urgent GP or 111 for blurred or double vision, flashing lights, a shadow across your vision, eye pain, or a red, painful eye. NHS glaucoma (Jun 2025): sudden intense eye pain with a red eye and halos (rainbow circles) around lights means call 999 or go to A&E, so halos and sudden severe pain moved to vision_loss (now). Double vision and the moving shadow were left out to avoid overlap with stroke and vision_loss; a clinician may want the moving shadow back here.
- **Sudden hearing loss in one or both ears, sometimes with ringing, ear fullness or dizziness** (`hearing_loss`): <https://www.nidcd.nih.gov/health/sudden-deafness>, <https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2017/020895s048lbl.pdf>  
  Sexual-health drugs (sildenafil, tadalafil). NIDCD/NIH: treat sudden deafness as a medical emergency and see a doctor immediately; treatment delayed more than 2 to 4 weeks is less likely to help. The Viagra label (12/2017): stop taking it and contact a doctor right away for sudden hearing loss. Today, not now, because neither source points to 911 or the ER and the treatment window is days. Borderline under the owner rule, since NIDCD says "medical emergency".
- **Episodes of shakiness, sweating or confusion that ease after eating** (`low_sugar`): <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/hypoglycaemia/>, <https://medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000085.htm>  
  Kept at today, but no opened source supports same-day. NHS (Aug 2023) says see a GP (non-urgent) for repeated lows, or for low-sugar symptoms without diabetes, which points to week. MedlinePlus covers treating an episode, not when to book a visit. Not lowered because only one source addressed it (the owner rule needs clear, consistent support to lower). Confusion during episodes, and lows on insulin or sulfonylureas plus a GLP-1, argue for prompt medicine review.
- **Belly pain under the right ribs or in the upper middle that lasts more than 30 minutes** (`gallbladder`): <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/gallstones/symptoms>, <https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/217806s042lbl.pdf>  
  GLP-1 medicines. NHS gallstones (Aug 2025): urgent GP or 111 for tummy pain lasting more than 30 minutes; pain is often in the middle or under the right ribs, with nausea. NHS puts fever or shivering, yellow skin or eyes, sudden severe pain, or pain to the back with vomiting at call 999 or A&E (all in the reworded belly_pain). Zepbound and Wegovy labels list gallbladder disease, including stones and inflammation, and FDA (Jul 2024) saw gallstones after compounded semaglutide overdoses.

## Book a visit this week

- **Bruising more easily than usual, or nosebleeds or bleeding gums that keep happening** (`bruising`): <https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003235.htm>, <https://www.mayoclinic.org/easy-bruising/ART-20045762>  
  LOWER (the only proposed lowering). Sources consistently treat this as a routine visit. MedlinePlus "Bleeding into the skin" (Apr 2025): contact your provider (no "right away") for sudden unexplained bleeding into the skin or bruising. Mayo: see your provider if you start bruising more easily, especially after a new medicine. NHS nosebleed (Dec 2023): non-urgent GP for regular nosebleeds or nosebleeds on blood thinners. Bleeding that will not stop, including a nosebleed over 10 to 15 minutes (NHS: A&E), is covered by bleeding (now). A non-fading rash with feeling unwell is in the new sepsis entry. Clinician check: some would want today for sudden, widespread tiny purple-red spots with gum bleeding (possible very low platelets).
- **New or worsening low mood, anxiety or irritability** (`mood`): <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/stress-anxiety-depression/low-mood-and-depression/>  
  Supported. NHS (Jul 2026): see a GP (non-urgent) if low mood lasts more than 2 weeks or you are struggling to cope. Crisis goes to the separate 988 card. FDA removed the suicidal-ideation warning from the Zepbound label in Feb 2026 (label history). Not covered: sudden, severe changes in mood or behavior, such as aggression or not sleeping for days (relevant to anabolic steroids and stimulants); a clinician may want a today-level entry.
- **Tiredness that lasts more than two weeks** (`fatigue`): <https://www.mayoclinic.org/symptoms/fatigue/basics/when-to-see-doctor/sym-20050894>, <https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/sleep-and-tiredness/why-am-i-tired-all-the-time/>  
  Supported. Mayo: make an appointment if two or more weeks of rest, eating well and fluids have not helped. NHS (Jun 2023): see a GP (non-urgent) if you have felt tired for a few weeks without knowing why. Mayo says call 911 for fatigue with chest pain, a fast or irregular heartbeat, feeling you might pass out, or unusual bleeding; those are separate entries.
- **Weight loss or gain you can’t explain** (`weight_change`): <https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003107.htm>, <https://111.wales.nhs.uk/Cancer/>  
  Supported for weight loss. MedlinePlus (Jan 2025): contact your provider if you lose more than 10 pounds or 5% of your weight over 6 to 12 months without a known cause. NHS 111 Wales: see a GP if you have lost a lot of weight in the last couple of months. No source was opened for unexplained weight gain. Fast gain with leg swelling or breathlessness can mean fluid and may need sooner care; not covered.
- **A new lump or swelling anywhere that doesn’t go away or keeps growing** (`lump`): <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/skin-abscess/>, <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/Moles>  
  Level supported. NHS skin abscess (Oct 2026): see a GP (non-urgent) for a lump that is getting bigger, is hard and does not move, or does not go away. NHS moles (Jul 2023): see a GP (non-urgent) for a mole that changes. Changing moles moved to the new mole entry, so the melanotan-specific sign is clear. A painful, red or hot lump is injection_site (today). OVERLAPS breast_testicle and the new neck entry.
- **A mole that changes size, shape or color, itches or bleeds, or a new dark mark that doesn’t go away** (`mole`): <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/Moles>, <https://www.skincancer.org/press/the-skin-cancer-foundation-issues-warning-regarding-melanotan-ii/>  
  Melanotan-type tanning peptides. NHS moles (Jul 2023): see a GP (non-urgent) for a mole that has changed size, shape or color, is painful, itchy, bleeding or crusty, or a new or unusual mark that has not gone away after a few weeks. The Skin Cancer Foundation (Jul 2026) warns Melanotan II is not FDA-approved, with case reports of melanoma and fast mole changes that make skin cancer harder to spot. Split out of lump; PT-141 (bremelanotide) also lists skin darkening (label not opened here).
- **A lump or swelling in your neck, a hoarse voice that won’t go away, or trouble swallowing** (`neck`): <https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/215256s030lbl.pdf>, <https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/217806s042lbl.pdf>  
  GLP-1 medicines (boxed thyroid tumor warning). The Wegovy (Jan 2026) and Zepbound (Feb 2026) labels tell prescribers to inform patients of thyroid tumor symptoms: a neck mass, trouble swallowing, shortness of breath, persistent hoarseness. Neither label gives an urgency, so week matches the general lump guidance (NHS skin abscess page). Shortness of breath is breathing (now). Overlaps lump.
- **A painless lump or change in a breast or testicle, nipple discharge, or new breast growth in men** (`breast_testicle`): <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/testicle-lumps-and-swellings/>, <https://www.nhs.uk/content-widgets/health-a-to-z/gynaecomastia>  
  Level supported for painless changes. NHS testicle lumps (Jul 2023): see a GP (non-urgent) for a lump, swelling or change in shape. NHS gynaecomastia (May 2024): see a GP (non-urgent) for breast growth, a lump or nipple discharge; anabolic steroids are a listed cause. Possible misread: "testicle changes" could take in sudden testicle pain, which NHS says means go to A&E immediately or call 999 (also pain lasting more than an hour). No entry covers that pain. The newer NHS testicle-pain page (Jun 2025) puts lumps with aching or discomfort at urgent GP, hence "painless".
- **New headaches that keep coming back** (`headaches`): <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/headaches/>  
  Supported. NHS (Apr 2024): see a GP (non-urgent) if you regularly get headaches. NHS urgent GP or 111 tier, not covered by any entry: headache with vision problems, with vomiting, triggered by coughing or exercise, or with jaw pain when eating. A clinician may want a today-level entry for these.
- **Loud snoring with pauses in breathing, or falling asleep during the day** (`snoring`): <https://www.nhs.uk/content-widgets/health-a-to-z/sleep-apnoea>, <https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/sleep-and-tiredness/why-am-i-tired-all-the-time/>  
  Supported. NHS sleep apnoea (May 2026): see a GP (non-urgent) if your breathing stops and starts, you gasp or choke in your sleep, or you always feel very tired during the day. Testosterone can worsen sleep apnea (product labels, not opened in this review).
- **Mild nausea, constipation or heartburn that keeps coming back or won’t go away** (`stomach`): <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heartburn-and-acid-reflux/>, <https://www.medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003120.htm>  
  Level supported. NHS heartburn (Nov 2023): see a GP (non-urgent) for heartburn most days, or with food sticking or weight loss. MedlinePlus abdominal pain: contact your provider for discomfort lasting a week or longer, or bloating over 2 days. Possible misread: on a GLP-1, "ongoing constipation" with vomiting or a swollen belly can be a blockage (emergency). "Mild" points those cases to the new bowel_block entry (now) and to belly_pain and fluids.
- **Joint or tendon pain that isn’t getting better after two weeks** (`joint_pain`): <https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/joint-pain/>  
  Supported. NHS joint pain (Feb 2026): see a GP (non-urgent) if pain has not improved after 2 weeks of home treatment. NHS urgent GP tier: a hot, swollen joint, or joint pain with fever (injection_site or fever cover these). NHS A&E or 999 tier: injury cases (injury_swelling).
- **Any symptom that lasts more than two weeks or keeps coming back** (`lasting`): <https://111.wales.nhs.uk/Cancer/>, <https://www.mayoclinic.org/symptoms/fatigue/basics/when-to-see-doctor/sym-20050894>  
  Level supported, but no source gives a general "2 weeks" rule. NHS 111 Wales uses 3 weeks for cough, bloating and bowel changes. Mayo (fatigue) and NHS (low mood, joint pain) use 2 weeks for those symptoms. Two weeks is the more cautious synthesis. ## Not covered yet (for the clinician reviewer)

- Sudden or severe testicle pain (NHS: A&E or 999).
- Needing to pee but being unable to (urinary retention).
- Sudden big changes in mood or behavior that are not a crisis (aggression, days without sleep).
- Headache with vision problems or vomiting, or one triggered by coughing.
- Fast weight gain with swelling or breathlessness.
- Kept more urgent than one source suggests: `low_sugar` (NHS: non-urgent GP), `heartbeat`, `fluids`;
  `lasting` uses two weeks where NHS often uses three.
